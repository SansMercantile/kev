**Title:** One school, every way to learn.

---

Most online learning asks a child to sit still and watch. KEV asks them to walk.

Open KEV and you're standing in the Grand Concourse of an 11-floor campus.
Subject banners line the hall. Ride the elevator to the science wing and a real
lab is waiting — not a video of one. Walk up to the Human Atlas and 2,234
modeled structures come apart in your hands: explode the model, isolate the
heart, and read what it does. Walk further and an electric vehicle splits into
334 pieces you can study the same way.

This is what we mean by an all-in-one immersive school.

It's a campus you walk through. Four studio-grade labs — molecular chemistry,
N-body gravity, the living cell, the physics of light. An auditorium that
teaches the class you're actually taking, holograms included. A virtual library
where the textbook is one click from the bench. And sports: a Motion Gym that
reads your movement from the webcam to run dance and cardio games — analysed
on the device, never uploaded — with an optional smartwatch link for a real pulse.

It's a school that works offline. Every 3D model is precached, so a first-visit
device runs the full classroom with no internet. Notes and chat queue up and
send when you're back. Six languages, role-gated for students and teachers,
from first grade to final year.

And it goes further than the usual curriculum. In the Quantum Optimization Lab,
a real Ising solver replays a quantum-style optimizer cooling into a solution —
Max-Cut and Number Partition made playable. In the Dimension Explorer, a real
4D tesseract projects down to 3D so you can steer the fourth axis yourself.

We built KEV because children learn best when they can walk to class, take
things apart, and play — and because an online school should be honest about
what it is: real labs, real tutors, real offline. Not marketing.

KEV is live and free to start. Walk the campus: kev.sansmercantile.com

*KEV is educational software. Immersive scenes are teaching visualisations;
Motion Gym is on-device analysis, not medical monitoring.*
